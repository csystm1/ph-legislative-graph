Philippine Legislative Bills Graph

Co-authorship and committee-funnel analysis of Philippine Senate and House bills filed during the 17th, 18th, and 19th Congresses (2016–2025). Built from bettergovph/open-congress-data.

What this is

Three analyses over the metadata of 39,390 filed bills (7,761 Senate, 31,629 House):

  • Co-authorship network — who files with whom, community structure within each chamber-Congress.
  • Committee funnel — bills assigned to each committee and the fraction that became Republic Acts.
  • Temporal evolution — how the graph and funnel change across the 17th, 18th, and 19th Congresses.

The unit of analysis is the bill, not the vote: which legislative relationships the filing system actually encodes.

Key findings

  • House graphs are dense but weakly modular. Each 17th–19th House graph spans 301–316 legislators and 39,755–40,391 co-authorship edges, with Louvain modularity between 0.055 and 0.095. The signal is broad overlap between blocs, not clean partition into them. source: data/analytics/graph_stats.csv
  • Senate structure varies sharply by Congress. The 17th Senate's graph has only 4 edges and 83% of nodes isolated (no co-authorship edges at all); the 19th Senate's has 71 edges and 12% isolated. Same chamber, same co-authorship model — different data regime. source: data/analytics/graph_stats.csv
  • High modularity on a sparse graph is not evidence of coalition structure. Senate-18 has the highest modularity in the corpus (0.315) — but that rests on 23 edges across 24 nodes. It is what Louvain returns when there is almost nothing to partition. source: data/analytics/graph_stats.csv
  • Committee funnel rankings favor the committees that carry enacted bills. House-17 Rules touches 43% of its 1,035 assigned bills that became RAs; Appropriations touches 33% of 809. The funnel records where bills pass through, not where they die. source: data/analytics/committee_funnel.csv
  • Communities are not cross-Congress comparable. Each graph is clustered independently. A community id in house-17 is not the same coalition as the same id in house-19. Temporal comparisons read node/edge/community counts, not community identity. source: data/analytics/communities/*.parquet

Data and method

Source: bettergovph/open-congress-data, a community-maintained TOML corpus of Philippine legislative documents. Scope is the 17th–19th Congress (2016–2025); the 20th Congress is excluded as incomplete upstream.

Graph edges are co-authorship only. The primary sponsor of a bill is recorded as a node attribute, not as an edge — a distinction that keeps Louvain from clustering by filing volume. See the repository's Analyses section for the full rationale and known limitations.

Source, pipeline, and methodology: https://github.com/csystm1/ph-legislative-graph. Data from bettergovph/open-congress-data (17th–19th Congress, 2016–2025).
